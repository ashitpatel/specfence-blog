# SL1 results: the skill lint is not adopted

- **The pre-registration:** `2026-11-15-SL1-prereg.md` (14d0c95).
- **The owner's approval:** "Build and launch both", 2026-11-15.
- **The code:** `tools/dev/skill_lint.py` (the lint), `tools/dev/sl1/mutants.py` (SL-M), and
  `tests/test_skill_lint.py` (11 tests, passing).

**Verdict: do not adopt.**
- **SL-M fails:** 12 of 20 mutants were killed, against a bar of 90%.
- **SL-R fails:** 2 of the 4 held-out cases were reported, against a bar of two thirds.
- **What the decision rule says:** an SL-R failure alone means "do not adopt", so the one rework the rule allows for
  SL-M was not tried.
- **What happens to the lint:** it stays in `tools/dev/` as a diagnostic, because its list is still useful to read
  (see SL-L). It is not wired into the suite's output or the guard.

## Deviation D1: the smoke case failed under the rules as written

- **What failed.** The sentence that all four first-trial FC1 runs skipped (eight inputs between them; corrected from "eight FC1 agents") is "While you write the TLA+ spec, tag every step ...".
  It starts with a leading clause, so the first-word rule never counted it as an obligation, and SL-S failed.
- **The change.** A listed verb may now follow one leading clause (While, When, Whenever, If, After, Before, Once,
  For, Until, Unless or Where, then a comma).
- **What it was based on.** The change was made from the FC1 case alone, before SL-M, SL-R or SL-L ran.
- **The result.** SL-S now passes: the sentence is reported `unmarked-unprompted` at line 103 of
  `cleanroom-fc1-v1`. So does the sentence "When in doubt, tag".

## SL-M: 12 of 20 killed

- **The run:** 20 mutants on HEAD's skills, with seed 15, across five files.
- **The counts:** class (a) killed 1 of 6, (b) 1 of 4, (c) 5 of 5, (d) 4 of 4, and (e) 1 of 1.
- **Class (e) has only one mutant.** HEAD's nine skills contain only one sentence the lint counts as a `refusal`.
  The other four mutants went to (c) and (d), to reach 20.

**All 8 survivors had a marker on a sentence the lint does not count as an obligation.** Deleting or moving that
marker changes nothing the lint reports. The 8 split into two groups.

- **About three are descriptions, so a survivor is correct.**
  - change-guard's sentence explaining what the markers mean was drawn twice, once for (a) and once for (b).
  - "**No check** can prove a context was fresh" was drawn for (b).
- **Five are rules written as statements, and the heuristic cannot see them:**
  - "One input that guards several steps carries the same name and words on each (**checked**: the lint)";
  - "a mismatch is an `unclear` question too (**no check**)";
  - "Whether your sentences tell the situation faithfully is yours to get right (**no check**)", drawn twice;
  - the "allow" example (**no check**: ask with `choices`).

The second group is the cause. The skills mark rules that are written as statements, and the sentence rules only
see rules written as commands. A fix that recognised these five would be fitted to them, so none was attempted.

## What this says

- **Instructions in specfence's skills are written in too many forms for sentence rules to find them.** They appear
  as commands, as statements ("carries the same name"), as asides ("is yours to get right"), and as negations
  without MUST ("are NOT reasons to skip").
- **The markers sit on rules of every form.** So a lint keyed to sentence shape misses about half of what it
  should check.
- **The list still reads well.** About half of the unmarked hits are real unchecked instructions, and
  `model-slice` and `first-look` hold most of them. Reading that list by hand, gate by gate, is the cheaper way to
  find missing gates. The escape-hatch check from the same discussion, an audit of the gates themselves, was not
  part of SL1 and is still open.
- **Nothing is changed in the skills or the engine.**
